## Executive Summary

Bessent gave this interview live from the Treasury's Cash Room on March 13, 2026 — mid-interview, he was physically pulled away to the White House Situation Room by the President. The US is actively engaged in a military conflict with Iran, targeting missile manufacturing, air force, navy, and power-projection capabilities. WTI was ~$95/bbl (up from ~$60 at year-start, briefly peaking at $114), and a coordinated G7 + IEA 400 million barrel SPR release had already reversed the initial spike from $30 above pre-conflict levels.

Three policy pillars dominate Bessent's worldview: (1) the bond market as supreme arbiter — US Treasuries had their best year since 2022, the only G7 bond market with lower 10Y yields in 2025; (2) oil as duration risk, not level risk — Trump's energy dominance (record liquids production) and SPR release buffer the economy; (3) tariff reconstruction — AIPA emergency tariffs invalidated by Supreme Court, now being rebuilt under Section 301 / Section 122 authority, targeting a return to prior deal levels within 150-day windows.

The macro geopolitical thesis: Iran campaign clears path to lower oil prices post-conflict; Arab Gulf nations now alarmed about Iranian power-projection; G7 allies privately supportive; Latin America pivoting pro-US (Bolivia now ally, Chile re-aligning). US chip-stack claim: energy exporter, 70-80% of global AI compute, military and tech leads widening vs. China.

**PM implication:** Long US Treasuries — Bessent explicitly confirmed 10Y yield suppression is a policy objective, and the only G7 market to deliver it in 2025. Fade oil above $95 on duration thesis — SPR release + post-conflict supply normalization signal reversion to sub-$60 regime. Underweight EU sovereign exposure; US tariff 301/122 reconstruction is deliberate, not accidental. Watch Iran conflict resolution timeline as the key oil catalyst: Navy escort of Hormuz tankers imminent once air/sea superiority confirmed. Private credit: Bessent sees "wobbles" but no systemic risk; monitor regulated-entity linkages.

## 4) Regime & Scenario Analysis

**Base case (Bessent's view):** Iran conflict resolves within weeks; Hormuz re-opens; oil reverts toward $60; US economy bounces to 3% GDP; Treasury yields stay low; AI compute share rises to 70–80%; Latin America realigns pro-US; tariffs settle at prior deal levels.

**Bear case (not Bessent's view):** Oil duration exceeds 3 months; Fed forced to weigh inflation vs. slowdown tradeoff more acutely; private credit wobbles transmit to regulated banking; allies defect from tariff cooperation.

**Wild card:** Regime change in Iran. Bessent hinted the new Ayatollah may be injured or under internal threat ("We haven't seen him"). If regime collapses, geopolitical premium evaporates rapidly — oil gap-down risk.

**Historical analog Bessent drew:** 2008 WTI spike to $147 — resolved; Japan 2011 — resolved; ERM break — resolved. His framing: crises are temporary when fundamentals favor the resolver.

## 6) BS & Bias Filter

**Credibility high:** Bessent is speaking as Treasury Secretary with direct Situation Room access — this is primary source, not analysis. His bond market claims (best year since 2022, only G7 10Y lower) are verifiable and he'd have no incentive to fabricate. His oil duration framework is coherent and consistent with prior economic history.

**Watch for spin:** The Iran narrative is pure administration framing — "ahead of schedule," "cancer on the world," no acknowledgement of uncertainty or escalation risk. Hormuz "as soon as militarily possible" is a conditional, not a timeline. $11B war cost figure is almost certainly understated (excludes materiel, logistics, opportunity cost).

**Conflict of interest:** Bessent is simultaneously running Treasury policy AND commenting on it — he has every incentive to signal confidence. "No moments when the bond market scared me" is a PR statement, not an investment signal.

**Analytical gap:** No mention of dollar index implications, no discussion of what happens if Iran closes Hormuz proactively (mined or blocked), no China escalation scenario. Regime change framing came from the interviewer; Bessent deflected rather than denied.
