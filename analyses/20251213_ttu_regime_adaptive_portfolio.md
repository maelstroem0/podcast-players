## Executive Summary

Alan Dunne presents a framework for **regime-adaptive portfolio construction** that challenges the 60/40 orthodoxy. The core thesis: traditional labels (alternative/traditional, public/private) obscure true risk factor exposures. A typical institutional portfolio with 10-12 "diversified" buckets may have **80%+ exposure to growth and stable inflation**—leaving investors massively unprepared for tail scenarios like runaway inflation or deflationary deleveraging. The solution: allocate 40% risk to growth, 40% to **adaptive strategies** (trend following, macro), and 20% to true diversifiers—measured in risk contribution, not capital.

The **macro backdrop demands this shift**. We've exited a 40-year disinflationary regime (Volcker → 2020) defined by falling rates, central bank independence, and excess global savings. Now: debt/GDP at 2x 1999 levels, no fiscal surplus to cushion recession, $40T household equity exposure creating wealth-effect fragility, and bond markets globally (JGBs, Bunds) signaling structural capital competition from AI capex and defense spending. The 1990s parallel is **superficial**—productivity gains then came from PCs (supply-side), while today's AI spending is demand-side without proven supply benefits yet.

**PM implication:** The behavioral bias of optimizing hedge fund buckets in isolation (favoring market-neutral, multi-strat) vs total portfolio context (favoring trend, macro, commodities) explains chronic underallocation to adaptive strategies. Return stacking and portable alpha tools unlock this—but require rethinking risk budgets. Dunne's backtested adaptive portfolio outperforms 60/40 since 1990 primarily by **limiting drawdowns** (not beating bull markets), enabling superior compounding from a higher base.

## 2) Key Investable Insights

### Insight 1: Portfolio Labels Hide True Risk Concentration
- **Claim:** Most "diversified" institutional portfolios have 80-90% risk tied to growth and stable inflation
- **Asset Class:** Multi-asset, Alternatives
- **Mechanism:** Private equity, venture, private credit, long-short equity all correlate to growth in drawdowns
- **Expression:** Audit portfolio using factor exposure (not asset labels); reallocate to true diversifiers
- **Trigger to Exit/Flip:** If inflation falls sustainably below 2% and rates go negative again (return to pre-2020 regime)
- **Confidence:** High

### Insight 2: Adaptive Strategies as Midfielders, Not Just Defense
- **Claim:** Trend following should not be siloed as "crisis alpha"—it adds risk in uptrends, cuts in downtrends
- **Asset Class:** Managed Futures, Global Macro
- **Mechanism:** Rolling equity beta of adaptive portfolio swings from negative to >1 vs 60/40's static 0.6 beta
- **Expression:** Size trend/macro allocation for regime flexibility, not just tail hedging
- **Trigger to Exit/Flip:** Structural return to low-vol, low-correlation environment (unlikely near-term)
- **Confidence:** High

### Insight 3: 1990s Parallel is Misleading
- **Claim:** Today's AI boom is demand-side (capex spending); 1990s productivity came from supply-side (PC adoption)
- **Asset Class:** US Equities (Tech)
- **Mechanism:** Spending on chips/datacenters adds to GDP but productivity gains not yet materialized (McKinsey, St. Louis Fed studies)
- **Expression:** Skepticism on extrapolating 1990s equity returns; debt/valuation backdrop far worse
- **Trigger to Exit/Flip:** Clear evidence of AI productivity gains in corporate earnings and labor data
- **Confidence:** Medium

## 4) Regime & Scenario Analysis

- **Current Regime Fit:** Post-disinflationary transition. Not stagflation yet, but inflation sticky above target. Growth positive but financialized (wealth effect fragile).

- **Base Case (50%):** Muddle-through with elevated volatility; inflation stays 2.5-4%, Fed cautious, equities grind higher but with 15-20% drawdowns. 60/40 underperforms adaptive.

- **Alt Case 1 (25%):** Runaway inflation—tariffs + fiscal dominance + deanchored expectations. Bonds crushed, equities derated despite nominal growth. Gold/commodities/trend outperform.

- **Alt Case 2 (15%):** Deflationary bust—AI bubble pops, credit event, recession forces fiscal response that overwhelms debt sustainability. Deleveraging spiral.

- **Alt Case 3 (10%):** Return to Goldilocks—AI productivity materializes, inflation falls, soft landing. 60/40 and growth assets win.

- **Anti-Thesis Trigger:** Inflation sustainably at 2%, bond-equity correlation negative, Fed regains credibility
- **Tail Risks:** US fiscal crisis, yen carry unwind, China deflation export, geopolitical shock
