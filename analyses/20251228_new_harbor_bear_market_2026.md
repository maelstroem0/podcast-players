## Executive Summary

New Harbor Financial's John Lodra and Mike Preston expect **"highly likely" a true 20%+ bear market in 2026**, despite near-term bullish technicals. Their indicators are green, breadth is improving (Russell 2000, equal-weight S&P hit new highs), and they've nudged equity exposure from 45% to 47.5%—but this is tactical, not conviction. The setup they're watching: **500 S&P points up in 2 weeks** (~5-8% in a fortnight) would signal blowoff top and mandate defensiveness, not a reason to chase.

The **macro case for caution** centers on record valuations and passive investor complacency. A 60/40 portfolio from 2019 held passively is now effectively 80/20 stocks/bonds—at a time when US equities have "never been as richly valued." Three consecutive 20%+ years is statistically rare; a fourth is improbable. Add the Fed's renewed balance sheet expansion (semantics aside, it's stimulus), a Fed chair race to be most dovish, and Trump's "run it hot" rhetoric—these have "the markings of strength into early next year but an invitation to sell."

**Precious metals** (silver $72, gold at highs) are overextended but "saying something is different"—the 2011 pattern ($50 then collapse) hasn't repeated. Silver broke the triple top at $35, consolidated, broke $50, and kept going. New Harbor sees $100 silver and $5,000 gold as plausible targets. But **if metals are >20-25% of your portfolio, sell into strength**. The gold/silver ratio normalizing to 62 (from 102 at liberation day) supports trimming. On rotation: basic materials (metals/mining, not gold miners), healthcare improving; financials weakening; hyperscalers "becoming yesterday's news."

- **Anti-Thesis Trigger:** Inflation collapses, Fed cuts aggressively, earnings growth reaccelerates
- **Tail Risks:** Fed chair transition chaos, midterm election volatility, passive unwind (forced selling)
