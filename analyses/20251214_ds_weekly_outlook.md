## Executive Summary

The Fed's surprise $40B/month T-bill purchase program (RMO) through April signals **they overdid QT**, but Andy argues this isn't classic QE—it's a **bill market rebalancing operation**. The Yellen Treasury's 50% bill-financing strategy flooded the market, pushing repo rates above the Fed's target corridor. By buying bills, the Fed makes them unattractive relative to repo, forcing money market funds to shift allocations and compress repo rates back into range. This is "dovish but not super dovish"—comparable to BTFP's $120B injection during SVB, which sparked a 12% equity rally that later faded.

The **bond market's hostile reaction** is the key tell. Long-end yields sold off hard through Thursday lows on Friday despite the "QE-lite" announcement—if this were truly stimulative, bonds wouldn't be getting crushed. Equities finished down, crypto failed first (Bitcoin at channel support with "bare flag" risk), and the Hasset rally since Thanksgiving now has air underneath it. The Dec VIX-Jan spread collapsed from 240 to 140 on Friday's selloff, suggesting limited hedging and room for volatility to spike.

**PM implication:** This is a regime test. The consensus "Hasset = steepener, Warsh = flattener" trade is too cute—both Fed chair candidates are Trump lackeys until proven otherwise. Own wings (upside and downside optionality), don't press directional bets. Watch the 73K Bitcoin level (Saylor's average cost), bond futures at 115, and whether NASDAQ holds the Thanksgiving gap. If crypto and tech break support simultaneously, "it's going to get really weird to the downside."

## 2) Key Investable Insights

### Insight 1: RMO is Bill Rebalancing, Not QE
- **Claim:** Fed's $40B/month bill buying is designed to lower repo rates, not inject stimulus
- **Asset Class:** US Rates, Money Markets
- **Mechanism:** Oversaturated bill market (Yellen's 50% bill financing) keeps repo rates above corridor; buying bills shifts MMF flows to repo
- **Expression:** Front-end rates marginally supported, but don't extrapolate to risk assets
- **Trigger to Exit/Flip:** If SOFR consistently prints mid-corridor (success) or if Fed expands to coupon buying (escalation)
- **Confidence:** High

### Insight 2: Long-End Bonds Reject "QE" Narrative
- **Claim:** Bond market's Friday selloff signals this isn't stimulative—term premium still rising
- **Asset Class:** US Treasuries (10Y+)

- **Expression:** Sell ZB rallies near 115; target 114 or lower

- **Confidence:** Medium
