## Speaker Bio

**Stephanie Pomboy** is founder and president of MacroMavens, an independent macroeconomic research firm she founded in 2002. Former economist at ISI Group, she is known for early calls on the housing bubble, the 2008 credit crisis, and post-COVID inflation persistence. Her research focuses on credit markets, Fed policy transmission, and hard asset cycles. She publishes at macromavens.com.

## Executive Summary

Pomboy's core message: the Iran war and oil spike haven't created new problems — they've accelerated and exposed the structural vulnerabilities that were already in place. Pre-war, her two dominant themes for 2026 were rising commodity prices and rising bond yields, both driven by secular forces (Cold War for resources, AI energy demand, dollar diversification, global deficit financing stress). The war amplifies both vectors simultaneously.

The more urgent signal is in credit. Since the Fed began tightening in 2022, the expected credit deterioration has been playing out behind the curtain — in private credit, away from public market visibility. Four years of "extend-and-pretend" (continuation funds, secondaries, toxic ETF packaging) are reaching their terminal innings as write-downs mount and gates close. The critical underappreciated risk is spillover into BBB-rated investment-grade corporates — Oracle, Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile, CVS, Amgen, Boeing, GM — where one downgrade to junk triggers forced selling by IG funds, creating a cascade.

The bond market is sending an unusual signal post-Iran: 10-year yields rose +22bps rather than falling on flight-to-safety, confirming the global appetite for US Treasuries is near non-existent. The oil shock is also arriving precisely during peak tax refund season, which could fully monetize the price increase through consumer spending — feeding CPI persistence that constrains the Fed's ability to cut.

**PM implication:** Stay long hard assets — gold, miners, energy; these are the highest conviction calls. Avoid leveraged IG credit; monitor BBB-rated names (Oracle, Verizon, AT&T, GM, Boeing) for downgrade risk. Long volatility remains the most defensible hedge — still cheap. Fade legacy enterprise software only selectively; potential relief rally in beaten-down software (Oracle, etc.) if war proves short. Watch 10Y yield trajectory and HYG/LQD spread widening as lead indicators for credit cascade. Do NOT rely on public credit spread data to signal private credit stress — the opacity gap is real.

## 2) Key Investable Insights

**Top 4 Investable Ideas:**

1) **BBB-rated IG is the hidden time bomb** — Oracle, Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile, CVS, Amgen, Boeing, GM are all BBB. One credit event triggers forced selling by IG funds. Public credit spreads are NOT reflecting this risk because private credit opacity has masked the deterioration since 2022. Monitor for any of these names showing operating stress.

2) **Long volatility — still the right hedge** — Pomboy added vol exposure months ago. It is still cheap relative to the risk landscape. War, credit stress, oil — all argue for sustained elevated vol. Position before a credit cascade materializes.

3) **Hard assets: gold, miners, energy** — The secular thesis (Cold War for resources, AI energy demand, de-dollarization, EM diversification away from Treasuries) is only strengthening. Pomboy has been nibbling energy. Oil price volatility creates entry windows in quality energy names.

4) **Potential relief rally in beaten-down enterprise software** — If war proves short, software names (Oracle, others) that got destroyed on AI-disruption fears could see substantial bounces. The AI threat to enterprise software is overstated in the near-to-mid term; mission-critical code doesn't get replaced by yesterday's AI output.

## 7) PM Bottom Line

Pomboy's framework is clear and internally consistent: the war has not reset the thesis — it has accelerated two pre-existing forces (commodity prices up, bond yields up) while exposing latent credit fragility. The most underappreciated trade: the BBB IG cascade risk. Spreads haven't blown out yet, but the private credit opacity means the damage is already done — it just hasn't hit the public market yet.

**Highest conviction:** Hard assets (gold, miners, energy). Long volatility. Underweight credit, especially BBB IG. Cash is still large in Pomboy's own allocation.

**Key triggers to watch:**
1. 10Y yield trajectory — sustained move higher accelerates debt wall refinancing stress
2. BBB downgrade events (Verizon, AT&T, Boeing) — fire-sale trigger
3. Gasoline prices vs $3/gallon level — leading indicator for consumer confidence and Fed political pressure
4. Private credit fund gates / write-down announcements — rate of acceleration
