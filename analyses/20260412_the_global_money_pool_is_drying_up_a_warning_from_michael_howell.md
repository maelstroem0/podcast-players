## Speaker Bio

**Michael Howell** is founder of CrossBorder Capital and author of the Capital Wars Substack, the definitive institutional resource on global liquidity flows. He pioneered the quantitative framework for tracking global liquidity — the total financing capacity of the global financial system — and has built models used by some of the world's largest macro hedge funds. His 2020 book *Capital Wars* formalized the thesis that global liquidity, not GDP, is the primary driver of asset prices. Howell coined the "Global Liquidity Cycle" framework now widely referenced across macro finance.

## Executive Summary

Michael Howell delivers a structured warning: the global liquidity pool is entering a cyclical contraction phase, driven by three simultaneous drains — elevated oil prices, spiking US Treasury volatility (MOVE index), and a strengthening dollar — arriving precisely as the COVID-era debt maturity wall comes due. The convergence of these headwinds creates conditions analogous to prior financial crises, where too much debt piles up against too little refinancing capacity.

Howell's core reframe is structural: modern financial markets are not capital-raising venues but **debt refinancing mechanisms**, with ~80% of all primary financial transactions being debt rollovers. This means liquidity is not a backdrop condition but the *governing variable* for market direction — economics is downstream of markets, which are downstream of money flows. The GFC, COVID crash, and every major crisis of the past three decades share a common cause: debt refinancing demand swamped available liquidity.

The crisis signal Howell is watching is the repo markets. When financing capacity becomes strained, repo spreads bulge first — as they did at end-2025, forcing the Fed to restart balance sheet expansion via "Reserve Management Purchases" (QE by another name, adding ~$200B). Despite official narratives of QT, the Fed balance sheet is expanding again. Meanwhile, the Trump administration is running its own stealth monetization: issuing massive short-duration T-bills absorbed by banks, which is textbook monetary inflation — the mechanical explanation for why US M2 just hit an all-time high and gold is surging.

The yield curve call is contrarian and precise: consensus entered 2026 expecting bear steepening (long-end rising faster). Howell's model had bear *flattening*, with bull flattening to follow — exactly what is playing out, driven not by policy tightening but by real-economy demand drawing liquidity away from financial markets (capex, AI infrastructure spend, working capital needs, oil price effects).

**PM implication:** Long bonds (bull flattening positioning, buy duration). Long gold — monetary inflation from T-bill monetization is structural. Short/underweight risk assets leveraged to cheap liquidity conditions. Watch MOVE index and repo spreads as leading crisis indicators — stress in these precedes equity drawdowns. SOFR spreads widening is the first warning sign; a repeat of late-2025 repo strain at higher debt-maturity-wall volumes would force another emergency Fed QE. Iran war impact on GDP is modest (~0.5–0.75% of world GDP) but the liquidity drain from $40/bbl oil premium (+$120B drawn from financial sector per $10/bbl × ~4 standard units) is disproportionately large.

## Key Quotes

1. > "Money moves markets. Economics is downstream of markets and I would argue that geopolitics is downstream of economics."

2. > "Something like 80% of all financial transactions — I'm talking of primary transactions in financial markets — are basically debt refinancing transactions now."

3. > "Each $10 increase in the oil price probably cuts about 3% from global liquidity, which is a meaningful figure — certainly a lot more than the impact on GDP."

4. > "A 10-point increase in the MOVE index can take as much as 4% out of global liquidity. Collateral markets operate via repo haircuts... if Treasury market volatility starts to jump, the collateral multiplier will shrink."

5. > "It's not happening because policy makers are tightening. It's happening because the demands of the real economy are increasing — things like capex spend, working capital demands, higher oil prices."

6. > "What is being missed is that behind the scenes banks' holdings of treasury debt are growing two to three percentage points faster each year than M2 money supply. That's monetization. And that's why the gold price is up."

7. > "The Trump administration is issuing a lot of very short-term debt, either treasury bills or very short-dated treasury notes. Who typically buys that debt? Banks and credit providers. Economic textbooks will tell you: that's monetization, pure and simple."

## 4) Regime & Scenario Analysis

**Base Case (Howell's implicit read):**
- Iran war impact subsides relatively quickly → GDP hit limited to 0.5–0.75% world GDP
- Oil prices remain elevated ($40 premium persists) → Ongoing ~12% liquidity drag
- Liquidity cycle continues down through mid-2026 → Bear then bull flattening plays out
- Fed maintains stealth QE (RMP) → balance sheet expands, prevents acute crisis
- Gold structurally bid via monetization channel

**Bear Case (Financial Crisis):**
- Iran war escalates / oil spikes further → liquidity drain compounds
- COVID debt maturity wall hits in volume → repo market stress episode (2x late-2025 SOFR spike)
- Fed too slow or constrained to respond → credit markets seize
- Collateral multiplier collapse (MOVE > 150) → GFC-type cascade
- Signal: SOFR spreads, MOVE index, bond term premium all spike simultaneously

**Bull Case:**
- Iran ceasefire → oil drops back → $40 liquidity drag reverses
- Fed accelerates QE (RMP) → balance sheet expansion overshoots, financialization reflates
- Real-economy capex demand peaks → liquidity flows back to financial sector
- Bull steepening or full bull flattening rally in bonds and risk assets

**Key Phase Transition:**
Howell identifies we are in **bear flattening** (2H 2025–1H 2026), transitioning to **bull flattening**. The consensus was still positioned for **bear steepening** — that phase has already passed. Being one phase behind the liquidity cycle is the source of hedge fund losses in SOFR/rates trades.
